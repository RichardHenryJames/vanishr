Vanishr / Security

THE BOUNDARIES MATTER

Privacy, explained.

What Vanishr is designed to protect, what it cannot prevent, and what still needs independent review.

Encrypted on the client

Vanishr uses the official libsignal library for one-to-one encryption sessions and private-group sender keys. Text and image content is encrypted on the Android client before upload. The relay routes ciphertext and does not hold the client's private identity, session or image-decryption keys.

HTTPS protects the client-to-relay connection. End-to-end encryption protects content between verified clients. Neither makes a compromised phone trustworthy or hides every piece of communication metadata.

Vanishr is an independent project. Its use of libsignal is not Signal endorsement, an independent audit, or a claim of compatibility with Signal's private-group protocol.

Verify the person, not just the name

Username lookup is discovery only. Before sending or decrypting a direct message, the trusted client requires an independently verified, pinned contact identity. Compare the safety numbers over a separate authenticated channel. A changed identity blocks the conversation until it is verified again.

A username can change or be reused. Existing contacts are tied to stable account IDs and pinned keys, not just a name. Optional Google sign-in authenticates your account, not the person you are chatting with.

Disappearing is not unsaveable

Choose view once, one hour, six hours or 24 hours. Relay payloads and local message records have bounded deadlines; retries do not reset those deadlines. Read or delivered acknowledgements remove eligible relay payloads. Already-delivered local content follows its original expiry and viewing rules.

A recipient can still retain content. They could photograph their screen or use a modified client. Screenshot flags and expiry reduce accidental retention; they cannot prove physical erasure or stop a malicious recipient from keeping a copy. Delivery and read receipts are not cryptographic proof of human viewing.

Profile photos stay out of search

Your optional photo is stored in your account's encrypted local vault, not as a public server profile field. Both people must save and independently verify each other as direct contacts before the trusted clients exchange encrypted photos. Group membership alone does not grant that permission.

The client converts a selected image to a small square JPEG without source EXIF metadata. Received photo copies and sharing requests expire within 24 hours unless refreshed. Removing a photo or contact sends an encrypted update or revocation when possible; an offline recipient may retain a previously authorized copy until its existing deadline. A malicious recipient can keep a copy longer.

Remote Photos requires owner approval

Only an account assigned ADMIN on the relay can request access. The trusted clients require a saved, independently verified direct contact, and the addressed owner must choose Allow before a transfer can begin. Android controls full or selected-photo access; the app cannot bypass denied permissions. The ADMIN label alone does not expose a gallery.

Sharing uses an isolated official libsignal session with the existing pinned identities. The relay sees the participants, approval, timing and ciphertext sizes, not photo contents or gallery identifiers. At most one bounded encrypted packet is buffered per direction, with a deadline no longer than one minute or the session. The full gallery is never uploaded. Originals stay on the owner phone; requested bytes and thumbnails are held temporarily in memory on the receiving phone.

A persistent Vanishr is active notification offers End access. Closing the owner's chat does not stop an approved background session; locking the phone, ending access, signing out, removing the contact, permission loss or a disconnected photo channel does. A session lasts at most 15 minutes and never past its access-token lifetime. Killing or force-stopping the service cannot silently resume approval.

There is no gallery-count cap, but requests are paged and rate-limited. The viewer accepts originals up to 64 MiB and decodes a display image of at most 4096 pixels on its longest edge. Unsupported or larger images show an error. Android may redact media metadata or restrict the available photos. An authorized recipient or modified client could keep anything already received; ending access cannot erase their copy.

Private groups use verified-owner trust

Groups support up to 200 members, including the owner and pending invitations. Each member independently verifies the owner and explicitly trusts that owner to verify invitees. This is not independent verification between every pair of group members.

The owner controls invitations, removal and closure. Membership changes rotate the encryption epoch. The owner must come online to approve changes before sending resumes. The relay knows group membership, but group names, messages and sender keys are client-encrypted.

Your phone remains a trust boundary

Android Keystore protects encrypted local state with nonexportable AES keys. Backups and app-data transfer are disabled. Foreground content is cleared when the app backgrounds, and secure-window flags reduce screenshots on supported devices.

There is no separate app lock: someone holding your already-unlocked phone can open Vanishr. Every vault access requires a configured secure screen lock and an unlocked phone. Any unlock method accepted by Android can be used; Vanishr does not add a timed authentication prompt.

Release 0.3.9 makes a compatibility tradeoff on Android 14 and below: new keys omit Keystore's unlocked-device-required restriction to avoid documented biometric-unlock bugs. The trusted app enforces the lock boundary there, but Keystore no longer independently enforces it if the app process is compromised. New Android 15+ keys retain that additional restriction. Key authorizations are fixed at creation, including after an OS upgrade.

Existing encrypted records migrate through authenticated decryption and encrypted atomic writes. A blocked old key can need one final PIN, pattern or password unlock; Android can also require a credential after reboot or biometric lockout. Vanishr cannot bypass an old key's authorization or recover an invalidated key. Failed migrations preserve affected encrypted data instead of resetting the account.

Keep a strong phone screen lock. Rooted devices, OS vulnerabilities, malicious keyboards and a compromised app installation are not solved by message encryption.

Google and password sign-in are remembered using a separate encrypted renewal credential, not a saved password or Google ID token. One-hour access tokens renew automatically within a rotating 30-day window. Sign-out revokes the current credentials; device replacement, 30 days without renewal or a relay Redis reset can also require sign-in again. A stolen renewal credential can authorize relay access until expiry or revocation, but does not decrypt your Signal messages.

Sign-out preserves encrypted account state for that account's return. Uninstalling or clearing app storage deletes local encryption keys and cannot recover old chats. Update over the existing signed app.

What the service can see

The relay handles account usernames and chosen shared display names, password verifiers or Google subject mappings, device identifiers, public keys, group membership, routing information, ciphertext sizes and timing. The hosting and network providers can observe network metadata such as IP addresses.

Online, typing and last-seen status is shared only between mutually saved, verified direct contacts. The reader needs an authenticated live connection; Online and Typing also require the peer's active connection. The relay sees the temporary sharing audience, typing recipient and timing over TLS; presence metadata is not end-to-end encrypted. Online records expire within 12 seconds and typing within five seconds. Starting in 0.4.1, one latest foreground-activity time and its sharing audience are retained for up to 24 hours, not an activity log. Reads do not extend that deadline. Sign-out removes it; contact changes replace permissions on the next successful heartbeat, and changed device identities block access. Draft text is never sent. The phone keeps status only in memory and refreshes it within 12 seconds. A received indicator may briefly outlast a lost connection or removal, and is not proof of identity, an exact disconnect time or message delivery.

Optional push notifications carry a generic event and may include a random, short-lived routing reference, not a sender name, chat identifier, message or photo. Only the authenticated recipient can resolve that reference; the unlocked app must still find the matching verified message before opening the chat. Invalid or stale references return to the list. View-once content is never opened automatically.

The Android app has no advertising or analytics SDK. This public website has a separate, optional analytics consent choice.

Development status and evidence

Do not use this development build for real sensitive conversations yet.

Release 0.4.2 passed 63 JVM security tests, all 99 Android QA methods, Android unit tests, QA/release lint and the complete signed live workflow on an isolated Android 12 emulator. Remote Photos checks cover admin-only initiation, owner approval, isolated Signal sessions, thumbnail pagination, original-byte transfer after closing the owner's chat, notification revocation, expiry, sign-out and device changes. The signed live test used a temporary synthetic admin whose role was removed afterward, and synthetic photos that were deleted. Clear chat checks cover confirmation, local key/outbox removal, preserved contact verification and successful encrypted messaging afterward. Existing session, profile-photo, presence, group, phone-lock, actual FCM and notification-tap checks also passed. These are functional tests, not an independent security audit.

Physical-phone/OEM behavior and Android 14+ partial-photo-access/foreground-service runtime acceptance remain unverified. Physical biometric unlock and Android 15+ Keystore runtime behavior also need validation. Notifications default on for an unset preference but still require Android permission where applicable; existing Off choices are preserved. Independent security and licensing review, real-device backup/screenshot checks, actual Google-provider acceptance, physical-phone push and background restrictions, and sustained abuse/load testing remain required.

The release source archive includes the threat model, architecture, API and verification documentation. Known limits are part of the product description, not a guarantee that no other issue exists.
