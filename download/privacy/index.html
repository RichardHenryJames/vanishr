Vanishr / Privacy policy

UPDATED 2 OCTOBER 2026

Your privacy, explained.

This policy covers the Vanishr Android app, its relay service and this separate public website. Vanishr is development software, not an independently audited messenger.

The app and your choices

Vanishr provides one-to-one encrypted chats and invite-only groups. The app has no advertising or app analytics SDK, does not sell personal data, and has no cloud chat backup or browser chat client. The privacy and deletion contact is richardhenryjames64@gmail.com.

Account and safety controls: version matters.

__ACCOUNT_CONTROLS_NOTE__

Read the account deletion instructions and Terms and community standards. You can request deletion without having the app installed. This website never asks for your password, sign-in token, private key or chat content.

Account and sign-in information

  • The relay stores your username, optional shared display name, random account UUID, account role, device UUID and public identity/prekey material. These support sign-in, contact discovery, encryption setup and access control. A username or shared name is visible in authenticated lookup; it is not proof of identity.
  • For password accounts, the password is sent over HTTPS for authentication. The account database stores a password verifier, not a recoverable password. The app does not save the password for remembered sign-in.
  • For optional Google sign-in, the relay validates a Google ID token and retains Google's stable subject identifier. Vanishr does not store or import your Google email, name or photo, or retain Google access/refresh tokens. Google processes the sign-in under its own policy; signing in is not peer verification.
  • Relay access credentials expire after one hour. A separate, rotating remembered-sign-in credential has a maximum 30-day lifetime per rotation; its digest is stored by the relay and the credential is kept in the phone's encrypted vault. Enrollment and Google sign-in challenges last five minutes. These lifetimes do not extend message expiry.

Account/profile records and public device material are durable until changed or the account is deleted. Private contact nicknames and the owner's selected profile photo remain in the encrypted local account partition, not in the public profile database.

Messages, contacts and relay metadata

Official libsignal encrypts direct messages and private-group traffic on the clients. Private identity, session and photo-decryption keys stay on client devices. The relay carries ciphertext, not readable chat text or photos. Intended recipients can read what you send.

The relay still sees account/device IDs, public keys, sender-recipient relationships, group membership, ciphertext lengths, requests and delivery timing. Hosting and network providers see connection information such as IP addresses. End-to-end encryption does not hide those relationships or make the service anonymous.

Contacts are added by username, not by uploading the phone's address book. Ordinary contacts must be independently verified. Invite-only groups use verified-owner trust: members trust the verified owner to approve other members, rather than independently verifying every pair.

Online, typing and last-seen metadata is not end-to-end encrypted. It is shared with mutually saved, independently verified contacts. The relay holds online status for up to 12 seconds, typing for up to five seconds, and one latest foreground-activity time and sharing audience for up to 24 hours. It is not a permanent activity history.

Google notifications and other providers

When notifications are enabled and permitted, Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) processes a provider registration token and notification delivery/network information. The relay keeps the FCM registration for up to 24 hours per registration, refreshed while needed. Push carries a generic event and, for supported clients, a random opaque routing reference, not a sender name, account/chat ID, message, photo, private key or sign-in credential. The relay's reference mapping expires within five minutes or the message's earlier deadline; the FCM event lifetime is at most 60 seconds.

Firebase also processes installation/device information needed to deliver its service. No Firebase Analytics SDK is enabled in the app. Turning off notifications or signing out requests push unregistration; it does not retract metadata already processed by the provider. Provider retention is separate from the relay's TTLs.

Google sign-in and Firebase operate under Google's privacy policy and the applicable Firebase privacy information. The service's hosting providers process network and operational information to run it. Provider processing may occur in other countries. This policy does not promise a particular data residency.

The sideload app's update checker contacts the static download host without account tokens or identifiers. The host still sees the connection and request timing. A browser opened for downloads or policies is separate from the encrypted app.

Blocking, reports and contacting the operator

The 0.4.7 account-safety controls include Block user and Report user/content functions. Blocking is intended to stop new direct interaction. It does not erase delivered copies or mean you no longer share a group with that account. Leave a shared group or contact its owner if needed. These controls require the updated client and relay described above.

Reporting is optional. A submitted safety report contains a random report ID, reporter and target account IDs, a selected reason, submission/expiry metadata and optional message/group IDs. No message plaintext, photo or screenshot is attached. The admin moderation queue retains report metadata for up to 30 days from submission and may remove it earlier after review. Completed account deletion removes reports submitted by or targeting that account; there is no post-deletion safety-report retention exception. Deleting the pinned admin clears the entire undeliverable report queue.

New in-app reports require an enabled, pinned official admin account. If none exists, including after that account is deleted, the relay returns 503 safety_review_unavailable rather than claiming a report was submitted. You can use the email contact below instead. An enabled admin account is not a promise that a person is currently monitoring reports.

Reports require human handling; a submission is not a guarantee of action or continuous monitoring. See child-safety standards and escalation. You can also email richardhenryjames64@gmail.com without using an in-app report. Email is not end-to-end encrypted by Vanishr: the operator and mail providers receive your sender address and anything you include. It is used to handle your request, not for advertising. There is no automatic mailbox expiry implemented; the app's message/report TTLs do not apply to correspondence. Send only necessary non-content identifiers and ask the operator about correspondence deletion. Never send passwords, tokens, private chats or illegal images, or post personal information to a public GitHub issue.

Retention, deletion and limits

Choose view once, one hour, six hours or 24 hours for messages. Relay ciphertext has an absolute deadline no later than 24 hours after acceptance; acknowledgements can remove it sooner. Retries do not extend that deadline. The trusted clients enforce the original message expiry and view-once rules. Local encrypted account settings, contacts and keys are not ephemeral messages and remain until removed or the local account is deleted.

The deletion page explains how to request account deletion by email, including after uninstalling. In the 0.4.7 candidate flow, fresh account-only password or Google reauthentication and typing DELETE precede the initial deletion request. Before sending it, the app must save a random deletion proof and the original account UUID in encrypted local storage. Google reauthentication of a saved account is bound to its expected UUID, not treated as a new sign-up. On server completion, account credentials/profile, devices, public keys, durable membership/association data and reports submitted by or targeting the account are removed, subject to the receipt and admin reservation below.

If cleanup fails, the account can remain in DELETING state. Previous credentials may already be revoked and normal account use is unavailable. The app uses its saved deletion proof to check completion or retry an already-started deletion without a bearer sign-in token. That proof cannot start deletion of an active or different account. Local account data is removed only after an explicit DELETED confirmation for the original UUID; an interrupted request, a sign-in failure or a 401/404 response is not proof of deletion. Signing out alone preserves an encrypted local partition; it is not account deletion.

Deletion confirmation receipt: the relay stores only the deletion proof's SHA-256 digest, original random account UUID, PENDING/DELETED state and creation/expiry times in its database. This receipt survives account deletion and a temporary-store reset so the app can recover a lost completion response. It contains no plaintext proof, name, login credential, Google subject, message or photo. Its fixed lifetime is 24 hours from creation; status checks and retries never extend it. Access expires at that deadline, and a scheduled purge runs every minute while the service is running; a service interruption can delay physical row removal, not extend access.

Queued ciphertext cleanup and read denial remain bounded by existing lifetimes of no more than 24 hours; deletion does not reset them. Other people's already delivered copies follow their original expiry. In-app safety reports involving the deleted reporter or target are removed, not retained for a further safety window. If the sole admin is deleted, an immutable random admin UUID reservation may remain to prevent someone else becoming that admin; it does not preserve their profile, credentials or private keys.

Uninstalling or clearing app data deletes local keys, not the relay account, and cannot restore old chats. Account deletion is not physical flash/RAM erasure, remote wiping of another phone, deletion of a Google account, or a way to erase a malicious recipient's saved copies. Backups/app-data transfer are disabled in the trusted Android app, but compromised devices, recipients or operators remain outside these guarantees. Read the security model and limitations.

This website is separate from the app

The Vanishr project operates this public site to explain the Android app and distribute release files. It does not offer account login, chat forms or a message viewer. Fonts, icons and product images are served from this site. Product screenshots contain fictional test data.

The Android app has no analytics SDK or advertising. This website's optional Google Analytics setting does not enable tracking inside the app. Read the app security model for how accounts, messages, metadata and local keys are handled.

Hosting and network information

The site is hosted by Vercel. Delivering a page or download necessarily exposes connection information such as your IP address, requested URL, browser information and request time to hosting/network providers. Their operational processing is not controlled by the optional analytics switch.

There are no advertising embeds, social-media pixels or session-replay tools. Following a link to GitHub, Google or another external service is governed by that service's own policy. This site uses a no-referrer policy for outgoing navigation.

Optional Google Analytics

__ANALYTICS_STATUS__

When configured, Google Analytics 4 loads only after you choose Allow. Before consent or after No thanks, the site does not load the Google tag or send Google Analytics requests, including cookieless analytics pings.

With consent, analytics measures page visits and clicks on the public APK/source download links. It can report approximate geography, browser/device categories, sessions and source websites. Google receives network information during these requests and may process data internationally under its terms.

We do not send app usernames, email addresses, contact IDs, chats, photos or encryption keys to analytics. Page URLs are sent without query strings or fragments; referrers are reduced to the source origin. Advertising storage, advertising personalization and Google signals are disabled in the website tag. Consent-based audience data is incomplete by design because visitors can decline or block it.

See Google's privacy policy and Google Analytics data safeguards for the provider's processing.

Change your choice

The site stores your consent choice locally in this browser for up to 180 days. With analytics allowed, first-party _ga cookies may identify a browser for up to 180 days. These are not Vanishr account identifiers.

Use Cookie settings at any time to turn analytics off. Revoking consent removes the site's analytics cookies and reloads the page without the Google tag. It stops future collection in this browser; it cannot undo information already sent to Google. A Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track signal keeps analytics off. Declining does not restrict any page or download.

Website analytics is off until you choose otherwise.

Questions and updates

For privacy, account deletion or safety questions, email richardhenryjames64@gmail.com. General, non-personal bug reports can use the Vanishr project issue tracker. Do not put personal information, conversation details or credentials in a public issue.

This policy will be updated when app, relay or website data practices change. The date above identifies this version. Neither these policies nor functional tests certify legal compliance, Google Play approval or an independent security audit.
